# th-ncsa-be2567

OSCAL for Thailand's **Notification of the National Cyber Security Committee
Re: Cybersecurity Standards for Cloud Systems, B.E. 2567 (2024) (มาตรฐานความมั่นคงปลอดภัยไซเบอร์ระบบคลาวด์ของ สกมช. พ.ศ. 2567)** — the standard
governing government agencies, regulators and Organisations of Critical
Information Infrastructure that use public cloud services, and the providers who
serve them.

Gazetted 10 September 2024. By its own clause 2 it comes into force **two years
after publication in the Royal Thai Government Gazette**.

_Last updated: 2026-09-01_

## The catalog

76 controls in 13 section groups across the standard's two parts, plus three
front-matter groups — the notification's seven operative clauses, the nine
defined terms of clause 3, and the clause 4 minimum-requirements and
certification table.

**Each clause of the standard is two controls, not one.** Clause 5 addresses the
Cloud Service Customer and the Cloud Service Provider in separate table columns
and says different things to each, so each column is its own control:

    th-cscs-5.2.3.1-csc     Access to Networks and Network Services (Cloud Service Customer)
    th-cscs-5.2.3.1-csp     Access to Networks and Network Services (Cloud Service Provider)

Six of the 41 clauses address one party only and mint one control.

Every control carries `label`, `clause`, `section`, `standard-part`, `party`,
`party-title`, `source-page` and `minimum-impact-level` as props.

## The six profiles

The standard grades impact level and party independently, so the profile set is
their product:

| | Cloud Service Customer | Cloud Service Provider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Low impact** | `low-impact-csc` — 19 controls | `low-impact-csp` — 18 |
| **Moderate impact** | `moderate-impact-csc` — 28 | `moderate-impact-csp` — 27 |
| **High impact** | `high-impact-csc` — 39 | `high-impact-csp` — 37 |

39 + 37 = 76: the two high-impact profiles together are the whole catalog, and
they are disjoint.

Profiles are **cumulative by import chain** — `high-impact-csc` imports
`moderate-impact-csc`, which imports `low-impact-csc`, which imports the
catalog. Each file therefore shows what its level *adds*:

| Level | Sections it adds | What that is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 5.1.1, 5.1.2, 5.2.1–5.2.4, 5.2.8, 5.2.9 | policies, roles, HR, assets, access control, cryptography, acquisition, suppliers |
| Moderate | 5.1.3, 5.2.7, 5.2.10 | compliance, communications security, incident management |
| High | 5.2.5, 5.2.6 | physical and environmental security, operations security |

Each profile also carries its own certification regime — self-assessment,
attestation, or certification by an accredited body on a three-year cycle, plus
the ISO/IEC and CSA STAR certifications required of providers — in
`metadata.props[name=certification]` and `metadata.remarks`. Those are
obligations about evidence rather than controls to implement, so they are
metadata and no identifier is minted for them.

The `resolved/` catalogs are the catalog reduced to what each profile chain
selects, for consumers that would rather not resolve profiles themselves.

## Read this before relying on it

- **The source is an unofficial English translation.** Every page of it says so.
  The only text with legal force is the Thai gazette,
  <https://ratchakitcha.soc.go.th/documents/43184.pdf>. Every control here is a
  translation of a Thai obligation.
- **`metadata.version` is the translation version, not the standard's.** The
  NCSA reissued the English translation in September 2025: same 41 clauses, same
  clause 4 matrix, same 76 controls, and roughly 45% word-level similarity in
  clause 5. These artifacts are built from **v1.1**.
- **Low and moderate impact omit section 5.2.6 Operations Security entirely** —
  17 controls covering change management, backup, logging, vulnerability
  management and environment separation. That is what the clause 4 matrix says,
  and no footnote flags it.
- **The footnote "\*Without 5.2.5" on the low and moderate rows excludes
  nothing**, because 5.2.5 is not in either enumeration to begin with. It is
  recorded verbatim and changes no selection.
- **`in-force-date` is derived.** Clause 2 says two years after Gazette
  publication; the document's own date is "Given on 10 September B.E. 2567", so
  the prop says 10 September 2026. If the given date and the Gazette publication
  date differ, so does the prop.
- **Profiles carry no roles or parties.** The National Cyber Security Committee
  issued the standard; it did not author these files and has not reviewed them.

## Verification

The builder's stage 3 validates these files against the NIST OSCAL 1.2.3 JSON
Schema and XSD, checks YAML/JSON and XML/JSON equality, resolves every profile
import chain, compares each resolved catalog against its chain, and compares
every control back to the source PDF re-extracted independently.

**85 checks, 0 failures.** 76/76 statements verbatim, and both party columns
reproduced word-for-word in document order — 2,160 words for the customer,
2,208 for the provider. The same 85 checks pass against a build from the v1.0
translation.
